# SD Worx challenge: Pay transparency, before the law lands

## Context

SD Worx pays more than 6 million employees every month for over 100,000
organisations. Many of them are Belgian SMEs with 100 to 250 people, no reward
specialist and one HR person who also does recruitment, absence and the
Christmas party.

The EU Pay Transparency Directive, (EU) 2023/970, had to be transposed by
7 June 2026. Belgium missed that deadline: the federal government asked the
European Commission for six more months, and the Belgian text for the private
sector is still being discussed with the social partners. So our clients know
the rules are coming, but they do not yet know the exact Belgian version.

What the directive already makes clear:
- Every employer, whatever its size, will have to answer a worker who asks for
  their own pay level and the average pay levels by sex for workers doing the
  same work or work of equal value (Art. 7). The answer is due within two months.
- Employers with 150 or more employees must publish a first gender pay gap
  report by **7 June 2027, on 2026 data**. Companies with 250+ report every year,
  150–249 every three years.
- If a category of workers shows a gap of **5% or more** that the employer cannot
  justify with objective, gender-neutral criteria, and does not fix within six
  months, a joint pay assessment with workers' representatives follows.

Our clients' 2026 data is already three-quarters written. And the first employee
questions have started to arrive.

## The challenge

**How might we help an SME employer understand its pay gaps and answer
employees' pay questions correctly, fairly and on time, before the Belgian law
is even final?**

## Who you're building for

Our client **Vlaskaai Logistics NV** (fictional), a 180-person logistics company
near Antwerp. Their HR manager handles everything people-related, alone. Her
payroll is run by an SD Worx payroll consultant who looks after dozens of SME
clients like her. She now has four pay questions in her mailbox, in three
languages, and a board that asks: "Do we have a problem, and what does it cost?"

You may build for the HR manager, for the SD Worx consultant, or for both.

## What we give you

All data is synthetic. Treat it as if it were real personal data.
- `materials/employees.csv`: 180 employees with job category, level, contract
  fraction, base pay and variable pay.
- `materials/job_architecture.md`: the client's job categories, the criteria
  behind them, pay ranges per level, and a data dictionary.
- `materials/requests.json`: four employee requests received in September 2026
  (NL, FR, EN).

## What success looks like for us

- A real user can go from "a pile of payroll data and a question" to a decision
  or a reply they trust, in minutes.
- The tool tells the difference between a gap that is explained and a gap that
  needs action.
- Every number shown can be traced back to the data and the method.
- It is honest about what the law says today and what is still open.
- It would make sense inside an SD Worx service, where humans supervise AI
  agents and stay accountable.

## Constraints

- Use only the synthetic data provided (or data you generate yourself, clearly
  labelled).
- A human stays in charge of anything that affects a person's pay or is sent
  to an employee.
- Pay data is sensitive. Be ready to explain where each piece of data goes.
- Do not claim Belgian rules that do not exist yet.
