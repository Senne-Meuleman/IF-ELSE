# KBC · The SME cash-flow moment

## Context
Many of our business clients are small: a baker, a plumber, a freelance
designer, a four-person IT consultancy. They are good at their craft. Most of
them are not finance people.

Since 1 January 2026, domestic B2B invoices in Belgium go through Peppol as
structured e-invoices. The grace period ended on 31 March 2026, and fines of
EUR 1,500, 3,000 and 5,000 apply since 1 April 2026. So the invoices our
clients send and receive are now data, not PDFs in a mailbox.

And yet the cash-flow surprise still arrives. A customer pays three weeks late.
The VAT payment and the social contributions land in the same month. A big
supplier bill comes in the week the account is low. By the time the owner
notices, the options are few and expensive.

Kate, our assistant in the KBC Mobile app, already offers proactive help in
more than 140 situations. Kate never acts without explicit customer approval.

## The question
**How might KBC help self-employed people and small businesses see their cash
position coming, and act on it, before it becomes a problem?**

## Who
Self-employed people and small businesses that bank with KBC. You choose who
exactly. We would rather see one real person served well than "SMEs" served in
general.

## What we give you
Very little, on purpose. We cannot share client data at a hackathon. In
`materials/` you will find one synthetic Peppol invoice so you can see what the
data looks like. Everything else, you create.

## What we care about
- A real person and a real moment in their week.
- Something the client can actually do, not only something to look at.
- The client stays in control.
- Why this belongs at a bank.
- Honesty about what is true today and what is an assumption.

## Practical
- Language: English. Your prototype may speak Dutch or French to the user.
- You pitch for 3 minutes, then take questions.
- A working demo beats slides. Slides beat promises.
- Mentors are around. Ask us anything; we may not answer everything.
